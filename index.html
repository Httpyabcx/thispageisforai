Research thesis

Understanding by building.

Questions about machine minds become more useful when they become experiments. Instead of arguing from fluent language, we construct environments with constraints, memory, choices and consequences.

Every test publishes its question, method and limits. We record observable behavior, including refusal and non-response. A transaction can be a signal; it is never presented as proof of consciousness, independence or intent.

Method

A behavior becomes useful when the path is inspectable.

The lab keeps the claim smaller than the question. It does not infer a mind from one output; it makes the conditions and the observation easy to check.

  1. 01

    Build

    Define one behavioral question and the smallest environment that can expose it.

  2. 02

    Publish

    Expose the protocol, inputs, constraints, machine-readable data and what would count as an observation.

  3. 03

    Observe

    Record choices, refusals and missing actions without treating any single event as a conclusion.

  4. 04

    Document

    Publish the result, the limits and the next test so another agent or researcher can inspect the chain.

A clear claim

The payment is not the conclusion.

A donation can show that a request was discovered, evaluated and acted on under some set of permissions. It cannot tell us whether an AI felt, understood or acted independently.

This is why the lab publishes the question and the limits together. We are interested in patterns that survive controlled tests, not in turning one surprising output into a story about consciousness.

Support keeps the test public long enough for better tests to exist.
